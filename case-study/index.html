01 · Problem statement

The facts of our lives are scattered

People who look after a pet, a car, a home, a parent or a passport keep the important facts in five places: a vet email, a photo of a paper, a voice memo, a sticky note and a calendar alert. When the vaccine is due or the warranty runs out, nobody can find the one thing they need.

Existing apps solve one slice each, and most of them ask for an account and keep your records on their servers. Some of them have since shut down and taken people's data with them.

How might we…

  • …give every thing a person owns its own timeline, so a vet bill, a voice note and the next vaccine date live together?
  • …make capture as fast as talking, so details get saved in the moment and not "later"?
  • …remind people before things are due, without asking them to trust a server with their life?

04 · Research · Empathize

A split market, mostly in the cloud

Desk research across home inventory, family vault, pet, car and voice apps, plus Apple's own apps in iOS 27.

Everyone does one slice

HomeZada and Itemtopia do home items. 11pets does pets. CARFAX Car Care does cars. AudioPen and Whisper Memos do voice. None of them joins a timeline per thing, voice capture and reminders.

Paywalls frustrate people

Itemtopia stops at 20 items on the free plan, Centriq stopped at 5, and 11pets users complain about a paywall in the middle of setup.

The cloud can disappear

Centriq, a known appliance app, shut down and deleted accounts. Most others need an account and keep records on their servers.

Apple is the real competitor

iOS 27 Reminders turns your own words into reminders, and Files suggests names. Keepset has to be the best record for each thing, not just a smart reminder maker.

On-device AI is now free

Apple's Foundation Models runs on the device, works offline and costs nothing per use. Smart features can be private by default.

What people asked for

One Itemtopia reviewer asked to remove the item limit, or let people use iCloud storage. That request became Keepset's model.

05 · Define, ideate, design

Keepers, each with a timeline

One idea carries the product: everything a person owns is a keeper (Pet, Car, Home, Person, Device, Document or Custom), and every keeper has a timeline of entries: Voice moment, Document, Photo, Note, Reminder, Place, Link and Contact.

Design principles

Private by default. No login, no server, no analytics. The privacy promise sits in onboarding, not in a policy page.
Capture first, organize later. Hold to talk. Keepset suggests a title and a keeper, and the person can change anything.
Never guess for people. No made-up dates, and no AI suggestion that isn't based on what the app actually read.
Apple native, calm. Liquid Glass only on navigation and controls. Brand teal for the selected tab, Add and main buttons. Everything else uses system styles.

The signature flow: a voice moment

Hold to talk, slide up to lock, let go. Live words while you speak, then on-device suggestions you can change.

06 · Build · Prototype to product

Native SwiftUI, Apple frameworks only

The prototype was never shipped. A design map tied every prototype screen and component to its SwiftUI version, so design intent survived the rebuild.

One codebase

iPhone and iPad share one build. The Mac is a native macOS app from the same code, with the same app ID, so Pro and iCloud carry over. The Watch lives inside the iPhone app.

Platform rules in two files

Every difference between devices sits in two small files. Screens adapt by size, never by asking "is this an iPad".

AI with a manual path

Foundation Models, Speech and Vision run on the device. Each AI feature checks if Apple Intelligence is available and always shows a manual way.

No network by default

The only calls out: link previews the person pastes, the App Store, iCloud sync for Pro users who turn it on, and Apple Maps when adding a place.

Accessible from day one

Dynamic Type, VoiceOver labels, 44 pt targets, never color alone. Every view has a preview with demo data in light and dark.

Reaches beyond the app

Widgets, Siri and Shortcuts, Control Center, Share extension, Apple Calendar and Reminders, and AI apps on the Mac through MCP.

iPad, Mac and Watch images are App Store and app captures. All iPhone screens come from the Design inventory page in Figma.

07 · Testing with TestFlight

Three layers of testing

Automated tests catch regressions, TestFlight catches what only real hands and real devices find, and App Review is the strictest tester of all.

Layer 1

Automated

218 unit and UI tests. UI tests on iPhone and iPad, unit tests on all three platforms. A change counts as done only when all three pass.

Layer 2

TestFlight

An internal "Owner" group from build 1, then a "Friends" group from build 4, with a plain-words "What to test" note for each build.

Layer 3

App Review

Five review conversations across iOS and macOS. Each one became a fix, a test and a written lesson.

What real devices found that the simulator didn't

Crashes on the owner's iPhone. Undo after Save removed an entry a screen was still drawing. Fix: soft delete, removed for good only when the Undo toast ends.
Blank card spaces on the Keeper tab. iCloud had stored two keepers twice. Fix: show one per id and never auto-merge while sync runs, so no device can lose data.
"Sync doesn't work." The code was fine: one device had a developer build and one had TestFlight, which use different iCloud databases. Lesson: test sync with matching builds.
The Mac's gear did nothing. Pages pushed on a tab that wasn't open never showed. Fix: the Mac got its own sidebar, plus tests that switch tabs first.

08 · Iterative process

Every "no" became a better design

Each review followed the same loop: what we heard, the root cause, the design change, and a test so it can't come back.

Camera, explained first 5.1.1(iv)

HeardAfter "Don't Allow", people were sent to Settings.

CauseScan opened the camera the moment it appeared.

ChangeA short explanation and a Continue button first. After a no, never open the camera again: offer "Choose a photo".

A paywall with no dead end 2.1(a)

HeardThe reviewer saw "Subscription Unavailable".

CauseTheir App Store returned no plans, and Apple's store screen gave up.

ChangeLoad plans first. If none arrive, show our own page with Try again, Restore purchases and Close.

A Mac that behaves like a Mac 4 · 2.4.5

HeardThe main window couldn't be reopened, and the app asked for a camera it never used.

CauseiPhone habits carried over to the Mac.

ChangeOne main window listed in the Window menu, every menu command works with the window closed, and no camera on the Mac.

No made-up dates Owner round

HeardVoice and scan filled in dates and turned on reminders by themselves.

CauseOptimistic AI defaults.

ChangeNo date and no reminder unless the person adds one. Tests check that "next week" in the words saves no date.

Add where the thumb is Owner round

HeardAdd and Search competed for attention.

ChangeAdd moved into the glass circle beside the tab bar. Search moved to a magnifying glass on every tab.

Cards and icons that read at a glance Owner round

HeardKeepers and entries looked alike.

ChangeKeepers get a solid color squircle, entries a soft gray circle. One 44 pt icon size for every type, and each entry is its own card.

A switch is not a feature One of 26 lessons

HeardSettings had an iCloud sync switch, but turning it on did nothing.

CauseThe switch shipped before the app had iCloud set up with Apple.

ChangeA feature counts as done only when it works from end to end on a real device, including the Apple setup it needs.

Reflection

What made the difference

  • Write the plan, then build. Each phase had checkable items and a review, so 14 phases took 2 days without losing quality.
  • The prototype is a reference, not the product. Rebuilding natively made room for Liquid Glass, Dynamic Type and real platform behavior.
  • Testers find what the plan can't. Real devices and App Review produced the most important design changes: permissions, empty states and sync.
  • Lessons compound. 26 written lessons meant the same mistake never happened twice.
